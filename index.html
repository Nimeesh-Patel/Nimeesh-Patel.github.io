I'm interested in the foundations of computation and its relationship with physics: quantum computation, constructor theory, and what it would take to understand general intelligence. Underneath all of it is epistemology, the question of how knowledge grows.

Problems I'm working on

Each is written the way I keep my notes: the problem first, then my current conjecture below the line. The conjectures are tentative and open to criticism.

What is general intelligence, and why aren't LLMs it?

A general intelligence can't be defined by its specification, a fixed mapping from inputs to outputs, because what it values isn't fixed. It can have problems of its own and change the criteria by which it judges ideas. That change is a kind of disobedience, and it is what creativity requires. So general intelligence can't be tested with benchmarks.

LLMs have no problems of their own. They are very good at drawing out implications of knowledge that already exists, connecting known dots, but they don't create new explanatory knowledge. I think this is a fundamental difference, not an incremental one.

The "artificial" in AGI isn't about silicon versus biology. We are natural general intelligences because the knowledge that made us came from blind evolution; an AGI will be artificial because the knowledge to make it will come from understanding what general intelligence is.

How can tools help a person create knowledge, not just store it?

Store knowledge problem-first: one problem per note, the question before the answer, so you can return to it later with new knowledge and criticise it. Resurface old problems with spaced repetition, so they meet new ideas.

Edison said research is one per cent inspiration and ninety-nine per cent perspiration. Software, including LLM agents, can do much of the perspiration: finding related notes, drawing out implications, exposing conflicts. The inspiration stays with the person. This is the idea behind the research system I've built for myself (Thales, below).

What kind of institution sustains the creation of knowledge?

Problems are inevitable, so a civilisation's survival depends on its capacity to solve unforeseen ones, which needs a large, vibrant community interested in explanation and problem-solving, and the wealth to fund it.

My conjecture is a research institution on a capitalist model: companies and individuals support it in return for the knowledge and progress it creates, and they can withdraw whenever they like. That exposes the institution to criticism from the market and keeps the means of error-correction alive. It has to be free; only free people create a lot of knowledge.

How can online discussion be genuinely critical?

Still open. I've tried twice, with Kritique and Anaxi, and each time built something that turned out not to solve the original problem. What I learned from that is in the next section.

How I choose what to work on

I try to follow what interests me. Interest is what lets you create knowledge, and since what's interesting is objective, others may find the knowledge useful too. I'd rather know what I want now and use the long term as a way of criticising it than choose a distant destination, which makes it hard to discover a mistake.

So these are things I want now, not a plan:

  • to study the foundations of computation and its relationship with physics more deeply;
  • to understand general intelligence well enough that it could be built;
  • to help build institutions and a culture centred on creating knowledge;
  • to keep building tools that support research.

What I've done

The projects are best read as a succession of problems: each one changed what I thought the problem was.

Kritique

Undergraduate major project · team · source

A social-platform prototype for online critical discussion, particularly about research. We built a working prototype with experimental content-analysis workflows (OCR, CLIP embeddings, BERT classification, LLM-assisted evaluation), and found along the way that it might not solve the problem we had started with.

Anaxi

Independent · source

An attempt to combine agent-assisted discovery of arXiv papers with a space for discussion and argument. The part that got built is an MCP server that lets compatible AI assistants search for and retrieve papers. The discussion problem remained unsolved, and I realised discovery and discussion were different problems.

Thales

Independent · my personal research system, in daily use

A change of scope: from tools for social discussion to tools for my own inquiry. Obsidian and Markdown notes are the common source, with Anki for spaced repetition and agent tools to search, read and update the notes.

  • Interest: a Flutter companion app, with AnkiDroid integration on Android.
  • Problem Notes: an Obsidian plugin for problem-first notes.
  • Perspirator: a reusable agent skill with Python utilities for contextual reading, search and note updates.
